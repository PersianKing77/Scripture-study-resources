    Herod's Temple Beta

    Prepared by Taylor Halverson, Ph.D.

    An explorable reconstruction of the Temple Mount as it stood in the time of Christ, c. 30 CE — the platform and its walls, the outer court and porticoes, the Court of the Women, the Court of Israel, the altar and laver, the sanctuary, the veil, and the Holy of Holies with the Foundation Stone within it.

    Every location is annotated from the New Testament, Josephus, the Mishnah and the Talmud, with notes where the sources disagree. Forty-three locations, one hundred sixty-two passages.

    Beta

    This model is a documented synthesis, not a recovered floor plan. It uses Mishnah Middot for much of the inner plan, Josephus for elevations and outer works, surviving perimeter archaeology where available, and named modern reconstructions for disputed placements. The inner sanctuary footprint has not been comprehensively excavated in modern times. Dimensions, decoration, rooflines and several event locations remain debated; alternatives are identified at the point of use rather than settled quietly.

    This visualization combines surviving perimeter archaeology with ancient literary descriptions and modern reconstruction. The destroyed inner precinct is not presented as a modern excavated plan.

    On the measurements

    Dimensions come from the Mishnah's tractate Middot, with the cubit taken at 0.5 m — a round figure within the range scholars propose — and cross-checked against Josephus. Small differences of a cubit or two are inside the margin of the sources themselves. Where the archaeology gives a figure directly — the Royal Stoa's columns, the width and tread of the southern stairway, the line of the walls — the excavated measurement is followed instead, chiefly as published by Leen Ritmeyer from Benjamin Mazar's Temple Mount excavations. Ritmeyer reckons in the royal cubit of 52.5 cm rather than 0.5 m, so his figures for the same building run about five per cent larger than the ones used here.

    Moving around

    • Orbit surveys the whole site from above: drag to turn, scroll to zoom. The compass keeps you oriented — the facade genuinely faces the rising sun.
    • Walk in puts you on the ground: WASD to move, drag or ← → to look, Shift to run. On a phone a drive pad appears at the corner.
    • Double-click the ground anywhere in Orbit and you are put on that spot on foot, facing the way you were already looking — the quickest way into a particular corner of the court. On a phone, arm Stand here under More and tap the place you want.
    • All 43 places lists every annotated location; ‹ Prev and Next › step through them in order, and Esc closes whatever is open.

    Seeing inside

    • Labels pins the names to the model; City adds Jerusalem around the mount — Olivet, Siloam, Golgotha, Hinnom — and can be turned off on a slower device.
    • Plan shows a small schematic of the precinct, north up, with a wedge marking where you stand and which way you face — useful when the courts start to look alike.
    • Morning cycles the hour. The first four — dawn, morning, noon, dusk — are stylised. The next four are astronomical simulations: the sun's altitude and bearing computed for the latitude of Jerusalem on selected representative dates for Passover, the Day of Atonement and Tabernacles — an approximation, not an exact recovered calendar day.

    Under More

    • Roofs and Cutaway lift the portico and sanctuary roofs and open the house, so the Holy Place and the Holy of Holies can be seen from outside. Everything is on by default: nothing in the model is hidden until you hide it.
    • People turns off the figures. They are there for scale: a court three hundred metres across reads as a diagram until there is someone standing in it.
    • Certainty colours the whole model by how much of it is actually known — green where there are standing remains or an explicit measurement, gold where the sources describe what no longer stands, red where they are silent or contradict each other and this is one defensible reconstruction among several.
    • Phases colours the platform by building period: the 500-cubit square described in Middot, the Hasmonean extension south of it, and everything Herod added north, west and south — with the two lines Ritmeyer's argument turns on, the seam in the eastern wall and the surviving step of the old western wall.
    • Measure turns the pointer into a tape: tap two points for the distance in metres and in cubits, which is the unit the sources argue in.
    • Sound adds a quiet crowd — loudest in the outer court, quieter through each boundary, and silent behind the veil. Off unless you ask for it.
    • The arrival runs ninety seconds from the ridge of Olivet to the Holy of Holies, narrated, and stops the moment you press Esc.

    Reading and teaching

    • Each panel carries the scripture passages for that place, a scholarly note, its sources, and “where the sources differ.”
    • Begin runs a guided sequence — the full tour, the Day of Atonement, a Passover pilgrimage, how a sacrifice was actually offered, Jesus in the temple, the apostles' temple, or Into greater light, which climbs the gradient of access from the open outer court to the Holy of Holies — visiting the places in the order the day required.
    • The same list carries sight lines: where the publican stood against the Pharisee, what a gentile could see from the lattice, the pinnacle of the temple, and the fact that no one then alive had seen the innermost room. Camera positions and a paragraph, rather than another list of places.
    • Handout opens the whole study guide, all 43 locations and 162 passages, ready to print or save as PDF — and while a tour is selected it prints that tour instead: its stations in order, with the narration and ruled space to write. The full handout also carries an index from the reading block to the stations worth opening in class.
    • Weekly Reading opens that index on screen: thirty reading blocks across the Old Testament and the New Testament, each with the places in this model worth opening for it. Tap a place and the model flies there. Because the curriculum renumbers its weeks every year, the index is keyed to the reading block rather than to a week number.
    • Copy link gives a link straight to one place, in orbit or on foot, to paste into a lesson.
    • Keyboard: M switches orbit and walk, [ and ] step through the current sequence, Esc closes, and the list of places takes arrow keys.

    Archaeology and scholarship

    • Excavated dimensions of the Royal Stoa, the southern stairway, the walls and the gates, and the building phases of the platform, follow Leen Ritmeyer, The Quest: Revealing the Temple Mount in Jerusalem (Jerusalem: Carta, 2006), and his published articles — the standard account of the mount's development, and the source of the two lines the phase overlay turns on.
    • Those excavations are the work of Benjamin Mazar and the Hebrew University expedition to the area south and west of the Temple Mount, 1968–1978, and of Ronny Reich and Eli Shukron for the Herodian street and the stepped ascent.
    • The geometry in this model was drawn from those published measurements.
