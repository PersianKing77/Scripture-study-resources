Authorship

Authorship and scope

The place descriptions, context essays, discussion prompts, confidence gradings and route traces were written and compiled by Taylor Halverson, PhD, as were the interface, the code and the visual design. Copyright in the text, the compilation and the software is held by the author.

The atlas covers 85 identified places, 61 events, 15 traced journeys and 53 weekly selections keyed to the 2027 New Testament reading year. Every place carries a graded identification — secure, probable, contested or conjectural — and a source list.

The atlas contains no photographs, no reproduced map plates and no generated imagery. Its only graphics are the map itself, drawn from the data credited below, and the diagrams and elevation profiles prepared by the author.

Citation

How to cite

Claims drawn from a place panel should cite the atlas. Claims the panel attributes to a named work should cite that work.

Scripture

Scripture

Scripture is quoted from the World English Bible and cited by book, chapter and verse. The atlas contains 177 quoted passages, each of one verse or a few verses. No continuous text and no complete chapter appears.

Modern scholarship

Modern scholarship

Each place panel carries its own source list. Where an identification is disputed, the panel names the competing candidates and the works arguing for each. The following are cited throughout:

  • Josephus, The Jewish War and Jewish Antiquities (Loeb Classical Library edition)
  • E. Stern, ed., The New Encyclopedia of Archaeological Excavations in the Holy Land (NEAEHL)
  • Y. Tsafrir, L. Di Segni & J. Green, Tabula Imperii Romani: Iudaea–Palaestina (1994)
  • J. Murphy-O’Connor, The Holy Land: An Oxford Archaeological Guide (5th ed., 2008)
  • R. Talbert, ed., Barrington Atlas of the Greek and Roman World (2000)
  • Eusebius, Onomasticon (ed. Freeman-Grenville, Chapman & Taylor, 2003)
  • D. N. Freedman, ed., The Anchor Bible Dictionary (1992)

Source lists give author, title and year. Page references are not supplied.

Tile service

OpenTopoMap (donation-funded, fair use)

Rendered map imagery is served by OpenTopoMap — no API key, no account, free for light, non-bulk traffic under its fair-use policy, licensed CC BY-SA 3.0. OpenTopoMap's server is best-effort and may be unavailable. The atlas temporarily caches a limited number of tiles that a user has viewed so recently studied views may reappear with poor connectivity; it does not prefetch regions or distribute a tile archive. Reuse of the map-rendering portion of any exported or printed image remains under CC BY-SA.

Coordinates and elevations

Facts · compilation © author

Coordinates are WGS84, verifiable against published survey data and excavation reports. Individual coordinates carry no copyright; the compilation, and the choice of which point on a multi-candidate site to plot, is the author’s.

Elevation figures and rift profiles are compiled from published topographic and survey figures. Ancient boundaries, road alignments and journey routes are interpretive reconstructions drawn from the literature and are approximate.
