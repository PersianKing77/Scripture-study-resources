What it is

Every place carries its own argument.

Tap a place and you get its scripture in full, its Greco-Roman and Second Temple Jewish context, what has actually been excavated there, why the terrain mattered to the people walking it, questions for discussion, and the literature the claims rest on. Then print it as a study sheet.

Search a reference

Type Mark 5, Jn 4:6 or Acts 10 and the atlas finds the places and events tied to that passage.

Fourteen layers

Herodian borders, Jewish and Gentile territory, Roman roads, the rift and its elevations, the sixteen ancient harbours of the lake, a day’s walk in rings.

CFM 2027, week by week

All fifty-three weeks of the 2027 New Testament year, each framing its own theatre of action — the sites lit, the routes drawn, everything else receding — and each linking straight to its lesson in the Gospel Library.

Measure and practise

Distance and days on foot between any two places, a chronology band, and a map-recall drill with the labels taken off.

Made for a room

Presenter mode raises type and thickens routes for a classroom screen. Study sheets and multi-place packets print clean.

Your own marginalia

Save places and write notes that stay in your browser — and print on the study sheet with the rest. Any lesson week prints as a handout, notes and all.

Why this one is different

Bible maps print every name in the same type.

Which quietly tells the reader that Capernaum and Emmaus are known to the same degree. They are not. Capernaum is fixed by excavation and continuous name; Emmaus has four candidates and two manuscript distances. This atlas grades every identification, marks the contested ones on the map itself, and names the competing candidates rather than choosing for you.

Secure

Fixed by continuous name, inscription, or excavation matching the ancient descriptions.

Probable

One candidate is widely accepted and unrivalled, but the proof is not decisive.

Contested

Two or more real candidates are argued in print — Bethsaida, Cana, Emmaus, Golgotha, the Praetorium. The panel lays out the case for each.

Conjectural

Rests on later tradition or inference. Scripture fixes no location a modern map can plot.

Every panel also lists its sources by author and title — pointers into the literature, so a teacher challenged on a location can say where the argument lives.

Corrections are the point.

This atlas makes specific claims about specific places, and a beta is where the wrong ones get found. If a coordinate, a date, a reading of an excavation, or a scripture reference is off, say so — name the place, quote the line, and say what it should say. Inside the atlas, Tools → Report an error carries a link to the exact view you were looking at.
